Polar coordinates

In polar coordinates the area element is dA = r dr dθ. The extra r is the key. Region: α ≤ θ ≤ β, r₁(θ) ≤ r ≤ r₂(θ). Use t for θ.

Try r₂ = 2 (disk), 2*cos(t) with θ from -pi/2 to pi/2, or r₁ = 1, r₂ = 2 (ring). With f = 1 the result is the area.

Riemann sums

The area under a curve is approximated by thin rectangles. More rectangles, better approximation.

Double integrals

The double integral ∬D f(x,y) dA equals the volume under the surface z = f(x,y) and above the region D.

How to compute it

Slice the region. Cutting along x = const gives a cross-section area A(x) = ∫ f(x,y) dy, and the volume is the sum of all those areas:

Setting the limits

  1. Sketch the region D in the xy-plane.
  2. Choose the order (dy dx or dx dy), which is the direction you slice.
  3. The outer limits are numbers. The inner limits depend on the outer variable.
  4. Find the inner limits by seeing where the slicing line enters and leaves the region.

Open the Visualizer and move the slice to watch the volume build up.

Try: x^2+y^2, sin(x)*cos(y), sqrt(x), pi
